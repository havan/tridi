# Air vent flange: design

Date: 2026-11-06. Status: approved by the user 2026-11-06. Open-area figures corrected afterwards from a geometry test while planning.

## Intent

Printed flanges that let air in or out of a drying box, such as a biltong box, and keep insects out. They bolt
over a hole cut in the box's side wall. They are the passive partner of the [`fanbox`](../../../projects/fanbox/)
project, which moves the air through the lid.

The side wall is plastic, about 2 mm thick, almost fully flat and about 50 × 35 cm. Flanges mostly sit at the
bottom of the side walls, and a box takes from 1 to about 4–6 of them depending on size and shape.

Every shape does the same job. People choose one for its looks, or because it suits their printer's bed. The
design is generated by code in the tridi repo, so anyone can build the variant they want.

## Goals

- One command produces print-ready files for any combination of shape, fixing and mesh below.
- Every variant prints on a common FDM printer without support material.
- With the mesh and bolts fitted, there is no route for a fly past the flange.
- The fixing can be chosen to suit the person: stronger, or less plastic, or fewer bolts.
- The summary tells people what to buy, what to cut, and how much of the opening stays open to air.

## Non-goals

- Hose or duct connections.
- Hoods, louvres or light baffles.
- Curved walls. The wall is treated as flat.
- Splitting a part that's too large for the printer. The build refuses it, as other tridi projects do, unless forced.

## Anti-goals

Any of these makes the design a failure even if it builds:

- A gap a fly can get through at the default settings.
- A variant that needs supports.
- A setting that silently produces a flange that can't grip the wall. With `--force` it's still reported in the summary.

## Options

Three independent choices. Every combination is valid.

### Shape: `round` or `slot`

| | Opening | Rim | Outside size, rim-bolted | Opening area |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `round` | 50 mm | 14 mm | 78 mm | about 1,960 mm² |
| `slot` | 190 × 21 mm, ends fully rounded | 14 mm | 218 × 49 mm | about 3,900 mm² |

The user set the round opening to half of an initial 100 mm proposal, and the slot's short side to half of an
initial 42 mm. *Builder's note, my call:* the 14 mm rim stays because it's what fits an M4 head beside the
opening, so the round outside is 78 mm rather than a true half of 128 mm. The two shapes are no longer equal in
area: the slot passes about twice the air of the round.

### Fixing: `two_piece`, `one_piece` or `centre_bolt`

All default to M4 countersunk hex-socket bolts and DIN 934 nuts, with the same hardware parameters as `fanbox`.

- **`two_piece`:** an outer and an inner printed piece squeeze the wall, with bolts through the rim. Heads sit
  flush in the outer piece, and nuts press into pockets in the inner piece. The wall needs a hole per bolt.
- **`one_piece`:** the outer piece only, to save plastic. Plain nuts and washers go on the bare wall inside.
  There's no collar. Bolts and heads as above.
- **`centre_bolt`:** both pieces, with bars across the opening joining the rim to a central hub, and bolts through
  the hubs only. The wall only needs the big hole, so nothing else needs drilling. There are no rim bolts.
  Requires two pieces by nature.
  - Round: one bolt through one hub, with bars out to the rim. Four bars by default, as in the user's sketch.
  - Slot: a row of hubs along the long middle line, three by default, each with a bar across the short side.
    *My call:* equal pitch with half a pitch at each end, so hubs sit at 0 and ±63.3 mm at the default size and
    the rim runs about 35 mm past the last bar at each end. The number of hubs is adjustable.
  - *My call:* with no bolts in it, the rim defaults to 8 mm instead of 14 mm, saving plastic and making the
    outside 66 mm (round) or 206 × 37 mm (slot).

Rim bolts: default 4 on round, spaced evenly, any number from 3. Default 8 on slot, spaced evenly along the rim's
centre line with one at each end, which gives the user's sketch: 3 along each long side and 1 at each end. The
slot takes any even number from 4. Odd numbers are refused because they leave the flange lopsided.

### Mesh: `wire`, `grid`, `honeycomb` or `slicer`

Available on every shape and fixing.

- **`wire`:** an open hole. A sheet of wire fly mesh sits between the outer piece and the wall, so its cut edges
  are hidden. Bolts poke through it. Because the mesh runs out past the wall hole under the clamped rim, no gap
  around it reaches the opening. No recess is cut for it: the rim stays flat.
- **`grid`:** the outer piece's opening is printed as a square lattice. Set by hole size, rib width, thickness and
  angle.
- **`honeycomb`:** the same, with hexagonal holes. Hole size is measured across the flats.
- **`slicer`:** the opening is left solid in the model. The user removes top and bottom layers and perimeters in
  that region, so the slicer's infill becomes the mesh. The build also exports a separate **mesh zone** shape
  matching that region, to load as a modifier so the slicer knows where to apply those settings. The README gives
  the settings for PrusaSlicer and for Orca or Bambu Studio, with an infill density aimed at holes of about 1.5 mm.
  *My call:* the solid region uses the same thickness parameter as the printed meshes, 1.2 mm by default, so the
  frame stays raised around it. Raising the parameter to the outer piece's thickness gives a flat plug.

Printed mesh defaults, all *my call* and untested on a real print:

| Parameter | Default | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Hole size | 1.5 mm, range up to 6 mm | Should keep house flies out, though not tiny fruit flies |
| Rib width | 0.8 mm | Two lines of a 0.4 mm nozzle. Must be at least the printer's nozzle diameter |
| Thickness | 1.2 mm | Six layers at 0.2 mm |
| Angle | 45° on grid | Matches the crosshatch in the user's sketch |

Holes over 2 mm build, but with a note that flies may get through. *My call:* there's no refusal, because people may
want a larger grille and no fly size has been measured for this design. The 2 mm threshold is an estimate.

Holes are whole cells only: a cell that would cross the opening's edge, a bar or a hub is left out, with at least one
rib width of plastic beside it. That keeps every part one solid with no slivers or loose fragments, at the cost of
some open area near the edges. At these sizes about 36–37% of a plain opening stays open, against 42% for an endless
mesh.

## Adjustable features

### Collar (`two_piece` and `centre_bolt`)

A short lip on the inner piece, fitting into the wall hole. It centres the flange and covers the hole's rough edge.

- `collar_height`: auto is wall thickness minus 0.3 mm. 0 leaves it out.
- `collar_wall`: 1.6 mm by default. The lip sits outside the opening, so it doesn't narrow it.
- `collar_fit`: the gap to the wall hole, 0.2 mm each side by default. The wall hole in the summary follows it.
- A collar as tall as the wall or taller is refused, because the pieces would meet before gripping the wall.

### Hub stop (`centre_bolt`)

A boss on the inner hub reaching toward the outer hub, so overtightening can't bend the bars far.

- `hub_stop`: the boss's height from the inner piece's wall face. Auto leaves a 0.5 mm gap between the hubs once the
  wall, and any wire mesh, is in place. A smaller stop leaves a bigger gap, so the bolt can squeeze the pieces
  harder. 0 leaves it out.
- A stop that would leave no gap is refused, because the hubs would touch before the rim grips.

## Other defaults

Parameters like `fanbox`'s, each with a range, CLI flag and preset support:

- **Wall:** `wall_thickness` 2 mm.
- **Thicknesses:** outer piece 4 mm, inner piece 5 mm (1.6 mm ledge, 3.2 mm nut, 0.2 mm room).
- **Holes:** as in `fanbox`, for FDM: 4.5 mm for the bolt, 8.4 mm head seat, 7.2 mm pressed nut pocket. The
  printer's `clearance` scales the seat and pocket the same way.
- **Bolt length:** worked out from the stack and rounded up to stock sizes, with the tip clearing the nut by
  1 mm. All default variants come to M4×12.
- **Hubs and bars:** hub about 14 mm across, to hold the head seat and nut pocket. Bars 8 mm wide. Both adjustable,
  with bar count on round choosing 2, 3 or 4.
- **Fly seal:** no printed feature. The README suggests foam tape or food-safe silicone under the rim on `one_piece`,
  whose wall isn't backed, and on `centre_bolt`, whose rim is only held through the bars, as the fan box does. *My
  call:* a `seal_thickness` parameter, 0 by default, is added to the stack so bolt lengths stay right when a seal
  is used.

All sizing checks follow `fanbox`: a clear refusal naming the parameter, and nothing written. For example: the head
seat reaching the opening or the rim's edge, a hub too big for the slot's width, or a rib thinner than the nozzle.

## Overruling a refusal

Added by the user after approval. `--force` builds anyway when a check would refuse, for someone who knows better than
the check. Each overruled check appears in the summary's notes.

- Overruled: every sizing check above, including the mesh hole limit, nozzle and hub stop checks, parameter ranges, and
  the printer fit check.
- Not overruled, because there's nothing to build: an unknown parameter, a value that isn't a number or one of the
  listed choices, or geometry the kernel can't make into one valid solid.
- *My call:* `--force` is on the flange only. `fanbox` and `shelf` keep their refusals, but the shared code honours a
  `force` parameter on any project that declares one.

## Printing

No supports and no brim. Each part is exported rotated for printing:

- **Outer piece:** wall side down, head seats up. Printed mesh therefore lies flat on the bed, on the wall-facing
  side, with the rest of the piece as a raised frame around it from the outside. *This replaces my earlier
  suggestion of printing the outer face down: seats facing the bed would overhang.*
- **Inner piece:** wall side up, so collar and hub stop face up and need nothing beneath them. Nut pockets are on the
  bed, with short bridges over them.

Material advice as for the fan box: PETG, or ASA with a heat source in the box, because PLA softens at 55–60 °C.

## Output

`pixi run build flange ...` as a new `projects/flange/`, with presets, README and tests as in `fanbox`, and a line in
the repo's README. Parts: `outer`, `inner` (unless `one_piece`), `mesh_zone` (only with `slicer`).

The mesh zone must line up with the outer piece when both are loaded into a slicer. The shared export moves every
part to its own bed corner, which would misplace it, so export may need extending. Whether it lands as a separate
file or inside the outer piece's 3MF is the builder's call as long as it lines up.

`summary.txt` adds:

- Wall hole to cut: its size, and the bolt holes where there are any.
- What to buy: bolts by length, nuts, washers on `one_piece`, and wire mesh cut size when `wire`.
- Air: opening area, area left after bars, and area left after printed mesh. With `wire`, a note that fly screen
  is typically 60–70% open. With `slicer`, a note that it depends on the infill chosen.

## Airflow at the chosen sizes

For reference, as the summary would report. A 140 mm fan sweeps about 13,000 mm².

| Variant | Open area with printed mesh | Flanges to match the fan's full area |
|---|---|---|
| Round, rim bolts | about 730 mm² (37%) | about 18 |
| Slot, rim bolts | about 1,390 mm² (36%) | about 10 |
| Round, centre bolt, four bars | about 330 mm² (17%) | about 39 |
| Slot, centre bolt, three hubs | about 1,040 mm² (27%) | about 13 |

These comparisons overstate the need: a drying box doesn't have to match the fan's full capacity. The smaller defaults
are the user's decision and all sizes stay adjustable. Check airflow on a first build, along with insect-proofing and,
on a long `centre_bolt` slot, whether the rim's ends stay tight against the wall.

## Left to the builder

- Module layout and internal names, following `fanbox`.
- How the mesh is generated. Thousands of boolean cuts are likely too slow, so building it as 2D faces and extruding
  is probably better. Default builds should stay reasonably quick.
- Exact wording of messages and summaries, preset names and the test split.
- Whether all combinations are built in the test suite or a representative sample with parameter checks for the rest.
